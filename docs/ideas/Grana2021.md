# Review: Grana, Mukerji & Doyen (2021), Seismic Reservoir Modeling

## Context and contribution

*Seismic Reservoir Modeling: Theory, Examples and Algorithms* by Dario Grana, Tapan Mukerji and Philippe Doyen was published by Wiley in 2021, runs roughly 272 pages, and is the natural modern successor to Avseth, Mukerji & Mavko's *Quantitative Seismic Interpretation* (2005). Where the 2005 book set out the conceptual workflow — calibrate rock physics, fluid-substitute, AVO-model, classify — the present volume provides the rigorous Bayesian-inversion machinery that has, in the intervening fifteen years, replaced the kernel-density and Gaussian-mixture classifiers of the older book in academic and industrial practice. The author team is unusually well-suited to this synthesis: Grana (Wyoming) is the leading current contributor to probabilistic petrophysical inversion and Bayesian rock physics; Mukerji (Stanford) is the bridge to the Stanford SRB lineage and to *Quantitative Seismic Interpretation*; and Doyen, formerly of CGG and the author of *Seismic Reservoir Characterization* (Wiley 2007), brings the geostatistical and industrial-implementation perspective.

The book's distinctive contribution is twofold. First, it gives a *unified probabilistic formulation* in which rock-physics, seismic and geostatistical models are each cast as conditional distributions and combined via Bayes' rule, with explicit treatment of the prior, likelihood and posterior at every step. Second, it provides *implementable algorithms* — pseudocode and accompanying MATLAB / Python code — for the full chain: analytical Gaussian inversion for the linearized case, Gaussian-mixture and hidden-Markov-chain inversion for facies-conditioned problems, sequential Bayesian simulation and ensemble Kalman methods for stochastic inversion, and Markov-chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) for the fully nonlinear case. It is the first textbook in which the practitioner can read the theory, see the worked example, and copy out the working algorithm on the same page.

A useful way to position the book is along three axes. (i) Relative to Mavko, Mukerji & Dvorkin (2020) *Rock Physics Handbook*, this is the *inversion* book: the rock-physics formulas come in as forward models, and the book's content is what to do with them once seismic data are introduced. (ii) Relative to Avseth, Mukerji & Mavko (2005), this is the *probabilistic-inference* successor: the rock-physics-template diagnostic remains, but the classifier becomes a proper Bayesian posterior with quantified uncertainty rather than a kernel-density estimate. (iii) Relative to Doyen (2007) *Seismic Reservoir Characterization*, this is the *rock-physics-aware* update: the geostatistical scaffold is similar, but the likelihood now flows through a calibrated rock-physics forward operator rather than through an empirical regression.

## Main ideas and structural progression

**Chapter 1 — Introduction.** The opening chapter frames the seismic-reservoir-modelling problem as Bayesian inference over a hierarchy of variables — facies, petrophysical properties (porosity, clay, saturation), elastic properties (Vp, Vs, density), seismic measurements — connected by forward models that are at each level partially known. The chapter sets the central distinction between *deterministic inversion* (a single best-estimate reservoir model) and *stochastic inversion* (a posterior distribution over reservoir models), and announces the book's position that, in any realistic exploration or production setting, only the latter properly quantifies the risk that drives decisions.

**Chapter 2 — Rock physics models.** A condensed but self-contained treatment of the forward rock-physics models that the inversion chapters will use. Granular-media models (Hertz–Mindlin, soft-sand, stiff-sand, contact-cement), inclusion models (Kuster–Toksöz, DEM, SCA), bound-based mixing (Voigt–Reuss–Hill, Hashin–Shtrikman), and the Gassmann / Brown–Korringa fluid-substitution machinery are all present, in the same notation as the *Rock Physics Handbook* and abbreviated to the parts actually used downstream. A practical addition is the systematic treatment of *parameterized rock-physics models with stochastic coefficients* — the contact-cement coordination number, the critical porosity, the dry-frame moduli — which become the latent parameters that the Bayesian inversion subsequently marginalizes over.

**Chapter 3 — Seismic and petrophysical inversion.** The methodological core of the book. The chapter develops Bayesian inversion in five steadily generalizing settings: (i) linear Gaussian inversion (the convolutional seismic forward model is linearized, the prior is Gaussian, the posterior is Gaussian in closed form — the textbook "Buland–Omre" formulation); (ii) Gaussian-mixture inversion, in which the prior is a mixture indexed by discrete facies, giving a per-facies posterior and a posterior facies probability at each sample; (iii) Markov-chain-prior inversion, in which the facies sequence is given a vertical Markov-chain prior that suppresses physically implausible thin-bed flickering; (iv) ensemble-based inversion (ensemble smoother, ensemble Kalman methods) for the case in which the forward model is too nonlinear for closed-form Gaussian inversion but linear-Gaussian update steps still apply; and (v) Markov-chain Monte Carlo for the fully nonlinear / non-Gaussian case. For each setting the chapter gives the formulation, the closed-form or iterative algorithm, the assumptions under which the formulation is exact, and worked numerical examples. The Gaussian-mixture chapter alone is the cleanest derivation in textbook form of what is now the most widely used petrophysical-inversion method in industry.

**Chapter 4 — Geostatistics.** A condensed but operational chapter on the spatial-modelling tools that the seismic-reservoir-modelling chapters use: variogram analysis, two-point geostatistics (Sequential Gaussian Simulation, Sequential Indicator Simulation, kriging), multi-point geostatistics (FILTERSIM, SNESIM, DeeSse) for training-image-based simulation, and object-based / process-mimicking models for fluvial and turbidite geometries. The chapter's emphasis is not on geostatistical theory per se — for which the reader is referred to standard references such as Chilès & Delfiner — but on the *interface* between geostatistics and probabilistic inversion: how a geostatistical prior enters Bayes' rule as a spatial regularizer, how it can be sampled jointly with the seismic-likelihood term, and how sequential simulation provides the conditional samples that the ensemble methods of Chapter 3 require.

**Chapter 5 — Seismic reservoir characterization.** Chapters 3 and 4 are fused into the operational workflow. The chapter develops full seismic-reservoir characterization as a single, hierarchical Bayesian-inversion problem: given seismic amplitudes, a wavelet, well logs, a calibrated rock-physics model and a geostatistical prior, infer joint posterior samples of porosity, lithology, saturation and elastic properties throughout a reservoir volume. The chapter treats the standard sub-cases — pre-stack seismic inversion to elastic attributes followed by rock-physics inversion to petrophysics (the "two-step" approach), versus the single-step joint inversion that goes directly from amplitudes to petrophysics — and shows that the joint approach properly propagates uncertainty whereas the two-step approach systematically under-estimates it. Time-lapse (4-D) inversion is developed in the same hierarchical framework, with the production-induced changes in saturation and pressure as the unknowns conditioned on differenced seismic data.

**Chapter 6 — Case studies and applications.** The book closes with a sequence of worked examples spanning conventional clastic reservoirs (a North Sea Brent-style example), unconventional shale reservoirs with anisotropic VTI rock-physics, carbonate reservoirs with mixed-mineralogy and pore-shape uncertainty, and CO₂-storage time-lapse monitoring as a non-hydrocarbon application that is increasingly central. Each case study runs end-to-end through the chain of Chapters 2–5 and is accompanied by code on the companion repository, so the case studies double as tutorial templates.

## Significance and limitations

The book's enduring value is that it consolidates fifteen years of probabilistic-inversion development — scattered across *Geophysics*, *Mathematical Geosciences*, *Computers & Geosciences* and SEG/EAGE proceedings — into a single, didactically organized text with implementable algorithms. The Buland–Omre linear Gaussian inversion (Chapter 3.1) and the Gaussian-mixture-prior inversion (Chapter 3.2) are presented in textbook form and citable notation for what is, in practice, the workhorse of modern industrial petrophysical inversion. The unified treatment of two-step versus joint inversion and the clean statement of what each gives up in terms of uncertainty propagation are particularly valuable; this distinction is now central to QI practice and is most clearly explained here. The MATLAB / Python companion code lowers the activation energy from reading the book to running the methods on one's own data.

Three limitations are worth flagging. First, the rock-physics side, although careful, is necessarily condensed; readers calibrating a non-standard rock-physics model (e.g., kerogen-rich shale, salt-affected carbonate) will need to combine this book with Mavko et al. (2020). Second, the seismic-physics side is essentially convolutional / linearized: full-waveform inversion paired with rock-physics priors is mentioned but not developed at the same level of detail as the linearized inversions, even though it is the area of most rapid current research (e.g., Asnaashari et al., FWI with rock-physics regularization; Aragao & Sava on FWI–QI integration). Third, the deep-learning / machine-learning extensions — variational autoencoders for facies priors, neural-network surrogate forward operators, generative-adversarial-network priors for reservoir geometry — are sketched in the closing sections but not treated at the level of detail that Chapters 3–4 give to the classical Bayesian methods. For these the reader will need to consult more recent literature (Sun & Alkhalifah; Mosser, Dubrule & Blunt; Liu & Grana). None of these limitations qualifies the central claim that, as of 2021, this is the standard reference for probabilistic seismic reservoir modelling.

For our project the book is the natural reference whenever the question is *how to invert seismic amplitudes for reservoir properties with quantified uncertainty*. Chapter 3.1 supplies the linear-Gaussian baseline inversion that any new method should be benchmarked against; Chapter 3.2 supplies the Gaussian-mixture facies-conditioned inversion that is the workhorse of modern industrial QI; Chapter 4 supplies the geostatistical priors that turn a sample-by-sample inversion into a spatially coherent reservoir model; and Chapter 5 supplies the joint-inversion formulation that properly propagates rock-physics and seismic uncertainty to the petrophysical posterior. It pairs naturally with Avseth, Mukerji & Mavko (2005) for the workflow-and-diagnostics view, with Mavko et al. (2020) for the rock-physics-formula catalogue, and with Tarantola (2005) *Inverse Problem Theory* for the inverse-theory foundations.
